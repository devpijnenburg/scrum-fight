Frequently asked questions

About Planning Poker in general, and about using ScrumFight with your Scrum team.

Planning Poker

What is Planning Poker?

Planning Poker (also called Scrum Poker) is an estimation technique where every team member secretly picks a card with their estimate for a user story at the same time. The cards are then revealed and the team discusses the differences. This keeps the first opinion from influencing everyone else.

Why use Planning Poker instead of just discussing it?

When people say their estimate out loud one by one, the first number anchors the rest. Voting at the same time gives every team member an equal voice, and differences between estimates point straight to misunderstandings or hidden work.

What are story points?

Story points are a relative measure of the effort, complexity and uncertainty of a user story. A 5 is roughly twice as much as a 2–3, not a number of hours. Teams use them to compare stories and plan how much fits in a sprint.

Should we use Fibonacci or T-shirt sizes?

Fibonacci (1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13 …) works well for stories in a sprint backlog: the growing gaps reflect growing uncertainty. T-shirt sizes (XS to XXL) are handy for rough estimates of epics or a new backlog. ScrumFight lets you pick a scale per room.

What do the ? and ☕ cards mean?

? means “I can’t estimate this yet”: usually a sign the story needs more clarification. ☕ means “I need a break”. If several people play ☕, take one.

What should we do when estimates differ a lot?

Let the people with the highest and lowest estimate explain their reasoning. Often one of them knows about risk or work the others missed. Then vote again. If the spread stays large, the story probably needs to be split or refined further.

Using ScrumFight

How do I use ScrumFight during backlog refinement?

Create one room for your team and share the code at the start of the refinement. Discuss a story, name the round after it, vote and reveal. Move on when there is consensus; talk when the spread is large. Saved rooms keep the same code, so the team can reuse them every sprint.

Can I use it in sprint planning?

Yes. Estimate the remaining stories for the sprint, and use the round history to see what has been estimated so far.

Does it work for remote and hybrid teams?

Yes. ScrumFight runs in the browser and syncs in real time, so it works alongside Teams, Zoom or Google Meet, on laptop and phone.

Can a Product Owner watch without voting?

Yes. Join as a spectator: you see the votes and results, but you do not vote and the countdown does not wait for you.

Do I need an account?

No. Enter a name and you can create or join a room right away. With an account you keep your rooms, statistics and badges.

Plans and privacy

Is ScrumFight free?

Yes. The free plan supports up to 7 participants per room and 3 saved rooms. Pro (15 participants, 20 rooms) and Premium (unlimited) are available for larger teams.

How long are rooms kept?

Guest rooms (created without an account) are deleted after 1 hour of inactivity. Saved rooms are kept until the owner deletes them, on every plan.

Which data does ScrumFight store?

Guests only enter a name. Rooms and votes are stored to run the session; guest rooms are deleted after an hour of inactivity. For an account we store your name, email address and a hashed password. ScrumFight sets no tracking cookies. Read the privacy policy for the details.
