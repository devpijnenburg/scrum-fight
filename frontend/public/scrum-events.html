Planning Poker for every Scrum event

Estimating is not an event of its own in Scrum: it happens in refinement and sprint planning, and it is worth looking back on in the retrospective. This is how ScrumFight helps in each of them.

🔍 Backlog refinement

Refinement is where most estimating happens: the team clarifies upcoming stories and sizes them, so the backlog is ready for the next sprints.

  • Create one saved room for your team and reuse it every refinement; the code stays the same.
  • Discuss a story first, then name the round after it and let everyone vote at the same time.
  • Consensus? Move on. Big spread? Let the highest and lowest estimate explain, then vote again.
  • Many ? cards mean the story is not ready yet: write down the open questions instead of guessing.

📋 Sprint planning

In sprint planning the team decides what fits in the sprint. Most stories should already be estimated; ScrumFight helps with the last ones.

  • Estimate stories that are new or changed since refinement.
  • Use round names and the round history to see what has been estimated in this session.
  • Re-vote a story when new information changes the picture.

🔄 Sprint retrospective

The retrospective is about how the team works, including how it estimates.

  • Look back at the round history: which stories had the widest spread?
  • Compare the estimates with how the work actually went, and discuss what you missed.
  • With accounts, personal statistics show trends such as consensus rate and favourite cards.

🌍 Remote and hybrid teams

Planning Poker with physical cards does not work on a call. ScrumFight does.

  • Share the room link in the meeting chat of Teams, Zoom or Google Meet.
  • Everyone votes from their own laptop or phone, at the same time.
  • Spectators, like a Product Owner or stakeholders, can watch without voting.

Tips for better estimates

  • Estimate relative to a reference story the whole team knows, not in hours.
  • Keep the discussion short: if two rounds do not reach consensus, split or park the story.
  • Let the Product Owner answer questions but not vote on the size.
  • Use the ☕ card: tired teams estimate worse.
